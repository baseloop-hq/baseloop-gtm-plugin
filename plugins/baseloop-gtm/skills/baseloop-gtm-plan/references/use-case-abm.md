# ABM

Use this recipe when the accounts are already named (this quarter's target list, a campaign's accounts) and the job is to put the whole buying committee at each one into the CRM, associated to the account, in bulk, with a start and an end. The question is not who to target but who to talk to at each account.

The shared rules own everything every use case does the same way, and this file does not repeat them: read [gtme-rules.md](./gtme-rules.md) for taking the request and building, and [gtme-rules-crm-and-delivery.md](./gtme-rules-crm-and-delivery.md) for working a CRM and delivering. What follows is what changes when the accounts are given and the job is coverage. The build skill cannot read this file, so every table, column, action key, gate, dedupe key and run order the plan needs goes into the plan itself.

## 1. The job

A team picks the accounts it wants this quarter, by name. Several people decide together, so the output is a committee per account: a person in each seat the deal needs, in the CRM, associated to the account, with the campaign on the record and the seats nobody filled named out loud. Everything is in bulk, and the work has a start and an end. The list is the campaign. When the campaign closes, the searching and the enrichment close with it.

Typical requests: "who do we talk to at each of our target accounts?", "get me the buying committee at these companies", "we have contacts at some accounts and nothing at the rest", "which roles are we missing per account?". What they want back, per account: a person in each seat, with a verified work email, in the CRM and attached to the account, tagged with the campaign; then the seats still empty, named; then the mobiles for the ones who get called. What kills it: the same person twice, once from the CRM and once from the search; a contact created loose, with no account on it; a search that kept running after the campaign ended and kept spending; a list of thousands of people that never left the tool.

## 2. Where ABM stops

- **Prospecting** 9.5 is one rep, a handful of companies, a list. Chain B (a list of companies, then the people at them) and chain A (a person completed to a verified email) are written in [use-case-prospecting.md](./use-case-prospecting.md) and cited here, never rewritten. When a recipe below cites a chain, read that file for the chain's own columns.
- **Outbound** ([use-case-outbound.md](./use-case-outbound.md)) is many accounts and one or two personas, starting from a description of who. ABM is the account list, a persona per role, into the CRM, per campaign. A workspace named for outbound that fills several roles per account is ABM by shape.
- **TAM sourcing** ([use-case-tam-sourcing.md](./use-case-tam-sourcing.md)) qualifies. An account on the list is in; a list that has to be qualified first is TAM sourcing feeding this.
- **CRM enrichment** 1.5 ([use-case-crm-enrichment.md](./use-case-crm-enrichment.md) "1.5 Role classification on every contact") owns buying role and department on the contact; ABM reads that output to select for the account. The whole CRM on a schedule is CRM enrichment; on request, per record, from a CRM flag is Prospecting.
- **Signals** ([use-case-signals.md](./use-case-signals.md)) watches the accounts while the campaign runs. Account-level facts go on the account, once, never once per committee member. A pre-call brief per committee member is rep-assist work. Neither of those two has its own recipe yet: build them from the shared rules.

## 3. Taking the request

The order is the one in [gtme-rules.md](./gtme-rules.md) "A request is a trigger, not a job". What ABM adds at each step:

1. **What the company sells names the seats.** Who buys, who signs, who influences: the committee is the whole recipe and it cannot be invented. No saved company profile exists for this agent, so take it from what the user said or ask it first.
2. **Check what is connected** (`get_connected_platforms`, and `connectionMode` and `connectionStatus` in `list_actions`): the CRM; a LinkedIn account with Sales Navigator, which makes the finder cheaper (the finder's mode is `optional`: it runs on Baseloop credits without one); the email and phone provider keys; the sequencer and Slack for the offers.
3. **Read the list by its values** (`get_table_schema`, then a few rows with `list_rows`): which accounts, and whether the rows carry a company page, a domain or only a name.
4. **Size it in the plan.** A search priced per person found over a named list, ending in CRM creates, always gets phases, each with the approval it waits on.
5. **Ask the open questions one at a time** with the harness's blocking question tool, at most four, each with its default marked recommended when it has one.

Rank the open questions in this order and ask the top four. Everything below the cut ships as a stated default the plan announces.

| Rank | Question | What it decides | Default when it ships unasked |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is the committee? | The seats, in their words, one role per seat, and the cap per seat: three or four seats at one to three people each is the usual shape. Which seat is the champion and which one signs, because they get different treatment later. The total to select per account, the function priorities, the seniority tie-break, any difference by segment. The search cap and the number handed to the rep are separate choices | Never defaulted; always asked first |
| 2 | When does the campaign start and end? | A name, a start date and an end date, on every row. Everything paid gates on the window | The end of the quarter |
| 3 | Do you already have contacts at these accounts, and how stale are they? | The CRM's own contacts are free and a found person is paid, so the existing ones are read and seated first. A CRM contact at a named account is often someone who left, which decides whether the position check is base or an offer | Read first; position check as base where the contacts are older than a year |
| 4 | What must not be touched? | The leave-alone test on the account (customer, open deal, owner, contacted recently, read back with the portal's own values) and the claim rule on the person when reps own contacts | Customers and open deals left alone; no claim rule |
| 5 | Which accounts, and where is the list? | A HubSpot list (`hubspot_companies_list_import`; `resolve_action_options` on `listId` returns the lists with their sizes) or criteria import, a CSV the user uploads in the app, or a company table already in the workspace (`create_rows` only for a handful of accounts the user types in). The list is theirs; qualification is not part of this job | The list as supplied |
| 6 | Should weak buyer coverage open an influencer search? | Whether a capped search per agreed influencer function runs where the buyer seats came back thin, and the coverage threshold that opens it | Not built; offered when the coverage word lands |
| 7 | Where does it land, and who owns each account? | The CRM is the default and the account owner is who hears about it; a sequencer and Slack are offers | The CRM, the account owner |

## 4. What changes for ABM

**The account is given, so coverage is the job.** Nothing here qualifies a company. Every paid step exists to fill a seat, and the measure of the build is not how many people it found but how many accounts have every seat filled. So the verdict lives on the account, not on the person.

**A seat is one search with its own cap.** One `li_find_people_at_company` field per seat: the titles in `currentJobTitles`, an exclusion as `{ "value": "Intern", "excluded": true }` on the same list, the cap in `maxLeadsPerCompany` (1 to 10). The cap is per field, so four seats at three people each is up to twelve people per account and the bill multiplies with it. The finder bills per person found, twice as much on Baseloop credits as through a connected Sales-Navigator-ready LinkedIn account: leave `useOwnAPI` out so the action picks such an account when the org has one, and never pin `useOwnAPIEnabled: false` on an org that has one. The finder upserts on the person's LinkedIn id inside its destination, so two seats finding the same person land once, which is one more reason for one people table. Where `searchRelaxed` is true in its fullValue, Sales Navigator ignored the title filter and returned people from the whole company: those rows are not seated until the seat classifier has read them.

**The free stream runs before the paid one.** The CRM's contacts at the account cost nothing; the search costs per person found. So the existing contacts are read and seated first, and each search is gated on its seat still being empty. Run the other way round, the build pays to find someone the CRM already holds.

**One people table for both streams, with the key on before the full run.** An existing contact and a found person are the same human often enough to matter, and a formula cannot see other rows. The finder writes its own columns into the people table (First Name, Last Name, Full Name, Role, LinkedIn URL, Company, Company LinkedIn URL); name the CRM send's mapping keys exactly the same, and `send_to_table` writes into those columns instead of creating new ones, so both streams fill one LinkedIn URL column. Run the first send or search on one row, which creates that column, then switch `set_auto_dedupe` on it (`keepRule: "oldest"`) before the full run. It also deletes duplicates already in the table and its deletions skip the Trash, so it is set while the table holds only that row, with the user's approval naming the table and the column, and never changed on a table that has run. A CRM contact with no LinkedIn URL escapes the key until the identity repair fills one; the second CRM check before any write still catches it. Two people tables cannot answer "is this person already ours".

**Classify the person, then select for the account.** CRM enrichment 1.5 owns buying role and department. Here those labels feed a complete eligible roster per account, ranked by the agreed function priorities, segment and seniority, with a stable person key breaking ties. Return Selected, Bench or Selection pending. A missing, truncated or misaligned roster is pending, never automatic selection. Refresh the selection after the candidate searches finish, because a ranking over the first arrivals cannot enforce the final account cap. Paid contact lookups and handoffs read Selected; the bench is a view on the same table. Classification, selection and contactability stay separate states ([gtme-rules.md](./gtme-rules.md) "Classification, selection and contactability are different states").

**The campaign has an end, and the end is a column.** A search left running outlives the campaign and keeps paying, silently, because nothing on the row says the window closed. The campaign name, the start and the end sit on every account row and travel to the people rows. Every paid step on both tables reads a Campaign open word computed from the window against a date the cycle rewrites, never a bare "today", which freezes on a row nothing rewrites ([pitfalls.md](./pitfalls.md) "Enriching recently contacted accounts"); the free steps keep running so a closed campaign still reads correctly. On close, the word flips and the searches stop themselves. Name the tables and views for the window too, so somebody opening the workspace months later sees that the list closed in April without reading a formula. A word on the row cannot stop a scheduled source, so closing the campaign is its own step in the plan, with an owner and a date: each schedule paused (read it with `get_table_schema` and send it back whole with only `enabled` changed), and `autoUpdateDependents` switched off with `update_table` on the tables whose search columns it drives, in the same pass.

**The employer check is a formula first and an agent on doubt.** A person found on LinkedIn states their own employer, and it is not always the account. Normalise both names in a formula, strip the legal suffixes, and send only the mismatches to a `custom_ai_agent`.

**A profile beats a research call for what a profile owns.** Where both can fill the title, the employer or the URL, the enrichment's value wins over research in the merge.

**Match back on one spelling of the account key.** People come back stating their own employer, so the people table finds its account by the company LinkedIn URL, then the LinkedIn company id, then the domain. Keep one canonical spelling of that URL on the account row, or two spellings of the same company URL need two lookups and two merge columns.

**Nothing found is written back to the account.** An account where no seat could be filled is a result the owner needs, not a silence. Write it as a CRM note on the account. The empty-account note is written only where every seat search actually ran without erroring; a throttled search is reported to the owner as pending, never as an account with nobody at it.

**Do not build the people leg before the write is agreed.** A people table with profiles, emails and phones whose write fields stay off puts nothing in the CRM: every credit buys a row nobody outside the tool can see.

## 5. The sequence, and the delivery

The accounts arrive and the campaign window goes on every row. The company page is resolved and proved, because a guessed slug points paid work at the wrong company: Prospecting chain B depth 1, cited. Then the free stream: the contacts the CRM already holds at the account, several not one, fanned into the people table with their record ids. Then the seats: one search per seat still empty after the free stream, capped, gated on the campaign being open and the page being proved. A seat that comes back empty gets one web-search call that reads the seat columns and fills only what is missing.

On the people table the chain forks once, on where the row came from, and never again. An existing contact arrives with a record id and is re-read live, then position checked. A found person arrives complete and is checked for employer. Both then run the same steps: the seat and department, the first CRM check, selection against the account roster, then the email for selected people who need it. Then the writes, with the account's CRM id, which the accounts table produced. Then the mobiles, only where they will be called. Then the account's own word: which seats are covered, which are missing. Then the extra facts, on the people who survived.

**Delivery** is the CRM, associated: contacts created or updated and attached to the account record, with the campaign on the contact, appended and never overwritten. A HubSpot multiple-checkbox property does that on its own: `hubspot_update_object` reads the record's existing selections and adds the mapped one without duplicates (keep the `metadata.keyType: "checkbox"` that `resolve_action_options` returns on the mapping). A static list is the other way (`addToList: true` with `listConfig.hubspotListId`, on the create and the update alike). Either rides in the same create and update, never a third write. Companies are created from the accounts table, once, never from the people table ([gtme-rules-crm-and-delivery.md](./gtme-rules-crm-and-delivery.md) "Companies are created from their own table"). The account record carries the coverage word, the missing seats, and a dated note keyed on the campaign so a re-run writes nothing twice; this is what the account owner reads. Offers: a sequencer campaign per account, and Slack to the account owner. Baseloop hands the records over. It does not send.

## 6. The three recipes

Two entry points into one campaign, and a third that hands the finished committee to a channel. Depth 1 is the base. Each next depth adds on the one before, in the order a team asks for it: complete the committee, put it in the CRM linked to the account, the mobiles, the committee view, the extra facts. Offered in that order, built on yes.

**Table count.** Two. The campaign accounts table, and one people table that both streams write into, with auto-dedupe on the person's LinkedIn URL (keep oldest) switched on after the first one-row send and before the full run. No third table for the net-new stream by default. A user who names the tables they want gets those, told once that the roster lookup and the LinkedIn URL dedupe work inside one table: a person present in both is ranked and capped by each table on its own and deduplicated by neither, and only the CRM lookup before create or update still catches the duplicate.

Cost below is shape only. Read the figure from `creditCostHint` in `list_actions` and from the action's guide (`get_action_schema`) when writing the plan.

### 6.1 Existing contacts at the accounts

The rows arrive with a record id, so there is no identity step; the CRM record is re-read live because the import's copy is a snapshot; the position check comes before any paid lookup, because a mobile for someone who left is the most expensive credit in the catalogue spent on nothing; and the write is an update, not a create, on almost every row. Taking the request adds two questions: how stale the contacts on these accounts are, which decides whether the position check is base or an offer, and what the team does with a leaver, which is a different sales conversation and never a silent skip.

**Depth 1, complete the committee.** The roles are also a tag written onto the contacts the CRM already holds, not only seats to search for: tag champions, influencers and economic buyers on the existing contacts so reps filter on the role in the CRM, and read the tag on a re-run to know which seat is empty. ### 6.2 Net-new per persona

Prospecting recipe 9.5 is the seed: the same accounts-plus-people shape, one rep, no campaign and no CRM ownership. Taking the request adds the seats and the caps, because the search is priced per person found and both answers change the bill. What is specific: the search is per seat and gated on that seat being empty; the found person states their own employer, so the employer check runs before the write; the fallback is a web-search call for a seat LinkedIn could not fill, and it reads the seat columns so it does not refill a seat that is done.

**Depth 1, what this stream adds on the accounts table**, after 6.1's shared rows:

| Field | Action | Gate | Cost | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|---|

On the people table, 6.2 rows take 6.1's chain with one row of their own: **Employer check**, a formula normalising both company names with the legal suffixes stripped, and `custom_ai_agent` only on a mismatch. Mismatch blocks the write; it does not silence the row. A seat can also be gated on another seat having found nobody, where one role stands in for another at a smaller company. A failed or unfinished buyer search is Search pending, not proof of weak coverage. A cap of three returns fewer than three per account where LinkedIn finds anybody, which is the number to cost with.

Remove a superseded set of find-people fields rather than leave it on the table beside the live set, both pointed at the same destination, and gate a second people source on the first, or both are paid for on the same accounts.

### 6.3 The audience for a channel play

The same target list, a different handoff. The committee is already in the CRM from 6.1 and 6.2. This recipe does not find anybody new: it makes the accounts and the people on the list matchable by an ad platform, and hands them over as a CRM static list the ad platform syncs. The sequencer sends; here the ad platform serves. Baseloop does neither. Two more channels take the same chain with their own field set, handed over as a view in the tool's import shape, because no native integration exists and the HTTP action carries no connection for a keyed endpoint: a landing-page tool (the logo, the company facts and the right person per account) and a direct-mail house (a verified office address and the recipient).

**Taking the request** adds four questions. Which platform, and which one first: the platform decides the columns and most of the bill, so never build for three at once; build the one they are running this quarter, and confirm each platform's own key spec before quoting it. A person on one platform costs an email or a profile URL; on another it costs a phone, which is the most expensive credit in the catalogue where the CRM has none; an account audience costs almost nothing. The key sets differ: one platform matches a person on first and last name, one of a work email, a personal email or the profile URL, and a country; another the same with a phone and a country; a third the same with a country code. Account audience or person audience: different jobs, and a team that only wants air cover over the list needs the first one and should not be sold the second. Who must never be in an ad audience: customers, employees, competitors, anyone who opted out, anyone a rep has claimed, read back from the portal's own values, asked before the plan and never an offer. Does the CRM already sync lists to the ad account: the handoff is a static list and nothing else, and a portal with no connected ad account ends in a list nobody collects.

**Table count.** Two, and they are the two ABM already has: the account audience is columns on the campaign accounts table, the person audience is columns on the people table. A table holding a filtered copy of the people who happen to be ready is a filter on a live table, and it leaves every earlier row behind. Every paid step reads the same Campaign open word. A user who asks for separate tables gets them, told once what the filter would have kept.

**Depth 1, the account audience.** On the accounts table, after ABM depth 1. Everything free except one enrichment, and it is the cheapest thing in this recipe.

**Depth 2, the person audience on one platform.** On the people table, after ABM depth 2. The committee is already there with a record id, so nothing is created and nobody new is found. Suppressed is pulled through the account lookup plus the person's own opt-out. Match keys missing is the free check that decides every spend below, and it goes here, not at the end. The profile URL counts only where it contains `/in/`; where the platform matches on it and it is empty, `custom_ai_agent` with web search finds it, then `enrich_contact` fills names and location where an essential is still missing. The write reads the merged first, last and country, never the enrichment's raw columns. Then Person audience ready, one formula per platform, Result (In audience, No match key, No country, Suppressed, Campaign closed, Pending), the list add on `hubspot_update_object` by the effective contact id, and Added on.

**Depth 3, buy the missing match key.** Only the gap, only on the platform that needs it, only inside the window. This is where the whole bill is: `waterfall_phone_enrichment` gated on Campaign open AND the platform matches on a phone AND no email match key AND the CRM's mobile empty by content AND first AND last AND company. A personal address from `find_personal_emails` (it requires the LinkedIn URL) is the last resort where no other key exists, and it never goes in the primary email property: the additional-emails property (`hs_additional_emails` on a standard portal; confirm with `resolve_action_options`), always.

**Depth 4, the second platform.** Offered only after the first one is running and the match rate is known. Adds the columns the second platform matches on, from the same shared location parser with extra outputs on, never a second table and never a second parser: one platform wants codes where another takes names, and that is the entire difference in width. Its own ready word and its own list id, so a person can be in one audience and not the other.

**Depth 5, keep it current and report the coverage.** Source last seen, from the import's runs against the cycle (`list_runs` for a failed fire, and `get_run_status` for the rows a fire created, `rowsCreated` in `sourceImportSummary`), not a formula (it sees one row and recomputes only when that row changes), plus a word when it is older than the cycle, because an audience that stopped refreshing looks exactly like one with nothing new. In audience now, comparing the intended state to Added on, so a re-run writes the difference and nothing else. Audience coverage per account over the people rows: how many of the committee are matchable, and which seats are not, written to the account as the word and a dated note where not already reported. The platform's own match rate, entered once per cycle, is the only honest measure of the recipe, and it does not come from Baseloop.

The shape is small: import the list, test the identifier the CRM already holds, fill only what the platform matches on, compute one word, update the record and add it to a static list in the same call. The bill sits in one place, the mobile, and whether a person needs one is decided by which platform is running, which is why the platform is the first question and the readiness check belongs in front of the spend. Three failures, each one line of config: a research step read back on the wrong extraction path leaves its paid columns empty and the write carries nothing from them (fix the path with `update_field` on the same column, never delete and recreate it); a write that sends the raw enrichment column instead of the merge next to it carries the unmerged values; and a build with no Suppressed word suppresses nobody. Test the no branch too: a sample where ready reads yes on every row leaves the "nothing found" half untested.

## 7. Offering the next depth

When a depth is built and verified, the plan or the build report ends with one closing line naming at most three next depths, deepest first, in this use case's order: complete the committee, put it in the CRM linked to the account, the mobiles, the committee view, the extra facts. Each names the table, the campaign, the accounts and the work in full, so it reads as a new request rather than a continuation of a finished plan.

After depth 1, the three are, deepest first, the committee view from depth 4, then the CRM write from depth 2, then the influencer fallback where coverage came back thin. After depth 2, they are the watch on the accounts while the campaign runs ([use-case-signals.md](./use-case-signals.md) 7.1), then the extra facts and a pre-call brief per committee member, then the mobiles for the seats that get called. The channel play, 6.3, is offered once the committee is in the CRM and never before. Offer, never assume, and never build the next depth because it seemed obvious.
